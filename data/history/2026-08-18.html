Detailed writeup

1. DHS Final Rule ending Duration of Status

Confirmed (official) · first tracked 2026-08-10 · last updated 2026-08-17 · re-checked 2026-08-18

For decades, F-1 students were admitted for "Duration of Status" (D/S) — their legal stay tracked their I-20 program dates, and a Designated School Official (DSO) could extend that end date directly in SEVIS for a compelling academic or medical reason, with no separate federal filing required. The new rule ends that. Beginning September 15, 2026, F-1 students receive a fixed "Admit Until Date" on their I-94, set to the length of their program as listed on the I-20 — capped at four years for undergraduate and doctoral programs (24 months for language-training programs) — plus a 30-day grace period to arrive and another 30 days after program completion.

Why this matters for someone who might need an extra semester: there are now two separate actions — (1) the DSO can still extend the Program End Date in SEVIS for a compelling academic or medical reason, for up to one additional year; (2) the student must separately file Form I-539 with USCIS to extend the actual admit-until date before it expires. A timely-filed I-539 buys up to 240 days of authorized stay while pending, but missing the filing deadline risks accruing unlawful presence. The I-539 EOS filing fee is reported at $420 if filed online / $470 by paper — a direct out-of-pocket cost that didn't exist under the old DSO-only SEVIS extension. See item 6 below: an incomplete I-539 package can now be denied outright rather than getting an RFE, so getting it right the first time matters more than before — and see item 2 below on USCIS's new authority to eventually mandate e-filing of this same form. USCIS will publish revised editions of Form I-539 and Form I-765 effective Sep 15, 2026, aligned to this new process. The current 08/28/24 edition of Form I-539 is accepted if postmarked or e-filed before Sep 15, 2026, but rejected on/after that date — only the new 09/15/26 edition is accepted from that point forward, so anyone filing right around the effective date should double-check which edition to use.

Practical risk: USCIS adjudication of Form I-539 is not instant, and the filing must be received before the current admission period expires. Combined with the deny-without-RFE policy (item 6), a late or incomplete filing is now higher-stakes than under the old D/S system.

New restrictions: F-1 graduate students are barred from changing programs, majors, or education levels once enrolled (narrow SEVP exceptions only); first-year undergraduates face a similar bar. DHS has discretion to postpone implementation of specifically these transfer/education-level provisions — not the core fixed-admission-period change — until as late as September 14, 2028, if it determines full implementation isn't feasible by the September 15, 2026 effective date.

Transition relief, with a hard outside limit: students in the U.S. under D/S before Sep 15, 2026 aren't immediately forced to file an EOS, and if they never leave and re-enter the U.S., they may remain until their I-20/OPT/STEM-OPT end date — but no later than Nov 14, 2030 (four years plus the 60-day grace period, measured from the Sep 15, 2026 effective date). If OPT/STEM OPT is part of the plan, Form I-765 (with I-539 bundled where applicable) must be filed by Mar 18, 2027 to use the simplified transition path. Leaving and re-entering the U.S. on or after Sep 15, 2026 ends the transition protections immediately — CBP will instead issue a date-specific Admit Until Date with only the new 30-day grace period.

Where things stand: NAFSA and higher-ed associations remain publicly opposed to the rule (see item 9 below for their latest enrollment-impact projection). NAFSA's dedicated legal-defense page confirms NAFSA and the Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration are formally preparing a legal challenge to the rule, focused on documenting concrete, immediate, and irreparable harms institutions can show — a firmer step than general public opposition, though as of this run (checked again 2026-08-18, fifth check) no lawsuit has actually been filed, and no CRA disapproval resolution has passed. As a "major rule," the D/S rule is also subject to a standard 60-day Congressional Review Act window in which Congress could introduce a joint resolution of disapproval; none has been reported. Treating the Sep 15, 2026 effective date as still on track. SEVP has scheduled a stakeholder webinar for Aug 31, 2026 (2–3pm ET, no advance registration required).

Virginia Tech specifically: Cranwell International Center (ISSS) publishes updates at international.vt.edu/immigration_updates.html, which states VT "is closely monitoring ongoing changes in immigration policy" — no VT-specific procedural guidance beyond that general statement found yet. This is a federal regulation, so guidance from peer international offices (Yale, Cornell, Pitt, Colorado, UIC, Northeastern, WSU) applies identically. Consistent advice: talk to your DSO now if there's any chance of needing more time, so the SEVIS extension and USCIS filing aren't both rushed against a hard deadline.

Suggested next step: contact Cranwell International Center before Sep 15, 2026 to get the program end date extended in SEVIS while transition relief still applies, budget for the ~$420-470 I-539 fee, and make sure any I-539/I-765 package is complete on first submission given the deny-without-RFE policy below.

2. NEW: USCIS interim final rule enables mandatory electronic filing — I-539/I-765 reportedly in scope

New today Confirmed (official) · first tracked 2026-08-18 · effective 2026-08-11

USCIS published an interim final rule (91 FR 51924, Federal Register doc. 2026-16313), effective August 11, 2026, that gives the agency legal authority to require mandatory electronic filing ("e-filing") of certain benefit requests. The rule itself doesn't immediately force any specific form online: USCIS can only mandate e-filing for a form once it has been available for e-filing for at least 180 days, and must give the public at least 60 days' advance notice before a mandate for a specific form or filing circumstance actually takes effect. Pending petitions are unaffected. Secondary legal-industry coverage lists an initial set of roughly 22 forms USCIS is expected to phase toward mandatory e-filing — reportedly including Form I-129, I-130, N-400, I-90, I-131, and, notably for F-1 students, Form I-539 (Extension/Change of Nonimmigrant Status) and Form I-765 (Employment Authorization).

Why it matters here: Form I-539 is exactly the form F-1 students will need to file for an Extension of Stay (EOS) once the Sep 15, 2026 fixed-admission-period rule (item 1 above) takes effect, and Form I-765 is the OPT/STEM OPT work-authorization form. If and when USCIS issues the required 60-day notice mandating e-filing for these two forms specifically, paper filing could stop being accepted for them — worth watching alongside the deny-without-RFE policy (item 6 below), since an incomplete e-filed package would carry the same denial risk as an incomplete paper one.

Confirmation status: confirmed directly via the Federal Register document itself and a matching official USCIS newsroom alert, "USCIS To Require Electronic Filing of Forms: Strengthening National Security." No form-specific 60-day mandate notice (for I-539, I-765, or any other form) has been published yet — only the enabling framework and the interim final rule itself exist so far. Public comments on the rule are open through Oct 13, 2026.

6. USCIS: deny-without-RFE/NOID policy (PA-2026-05)

Confirmed (official) · first tracked 2026-08-10 · effective 2026-08-05 · re-checked 2026-08-18

USCIS Policy Alert PA-2026-05, "Evidence, Requests for Evidence, and Notices of Intent to Deny," updates USCIS Policy Manual Volume 1, Part E, Chapters 6, 9, and 10, effective for requests pending or filed on/after August 5, 2026. It gives adjudicators discretion to deny a benefit request that lacks required initial evidence or otherwise fails to establish eligibility — without first issuing a Request for Evidence (RFE) or Notice of Intent to Deny (NOID). USCIS frames this as restoring pre-2021 practice to discourage frivolous or substantially incomplete filings that slow processing for everyone else.

Why it matters here: under the new fixed-admission-period regime, F-1 students needing an extra semester must file Form I-539 (Extension of Stay) — and OPT/STEM OPT transition filers use Form I-765. A materially incomplete package can now be denied outright rather than getting a chance to supplement the record, raising the cost of filing errors right when more students will be filing EOS applications for the first time, and right as USCIS builds out authority to mandate e-filing of these same forms (item 2 above).

Confirmation status: confirmed via the official USCIS newsroom alert and the underlying Policy Alert PDF directly on uscis.gov. No changes found today (fifth check).

7. SEVP warns DSOs of increased scrutiny on CPT authorization (BCM 2608-01)

Confirmed (official) · first tracked 2026-08-15 · issued 2026-08-12 · re-checked 2026-08-18

On August 12, 2026, SEVP issued Broadcast Message 2608-01, "Reminder of Liability for DSOs Regarding CPT Authorization," to every PDSO/DSO at SEVP-certified schools nationwide. SEVP says it has observed a rise in Curricular Practical Training (CPT) authorizations that don't meet regulatory requirements — specifically CPT that isn't an integral, required part of an established curriculum — and is increasing scrutiny of CPT approvals to ensure compliance. Schools found authorizing CPT improperly risk investigation and, in serious cases, loss of SEVP certification.

Why it matters here: this doesn't change the CPT regulation itself — DHS's July D/S final rule separately stated it "does not prohibit or eliminate Day-1 CPT" and "does not make substantive changes to CPT." But it signals stepped-up federal enforcement of an existing rule right as more students may lean on CPT for practical training during an extended timeline. Washington State University's Aug 7, 2026 international-office advisory — issued before SEVP's Aug 12 message, so an independent, earlier move — announced its ISSS will stop approving full-time CPT during the academic year starting Spring 2027, except for programs with an explicit mandatory-internship curriculum requirement. This is one university getting ahead of the issue, not a national rule; no similar announcement has been found from Virginia Tech's Cranwell International Center, whose CPT page still describes standard part-time/full-time eligibility with no dated policy update.

Confirmation status: confirmed via the official ICE/SEVP broadcast message PDF, corroborated by immigration-law reporting with no contradicting accounts. No new facts found today.
