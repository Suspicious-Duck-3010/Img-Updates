2. DHS Final Rule ending Duration of Status

Confirmed (official) · first tracked 2026-08-10 · last updated 2026-08-25 · re-checked 2026-08-25

For decades, F-1 students were admitted for "Duration of Status" (D/S) — their legal stay tracked their I-20 program dates, and a Designated School Official (DSO) could extend that end date directly in SEVIS for a compelling academic or medical reason, with no separate federal filing required. The new rule ends that. Beginning September 15, 2026, F-1 students receive a fixed "Admit Until Date" on their I-94, set to the length of their program as listed on the I-20 — capped at four years for undergraduate and doctoral programs (24 months for language-training programs) — plus a 30-day grace period to arrive and another 30 days after program completion.

Why this matters for someone who might need an extra semester: there are now two separate actions — (1) the DSO can still extend the Program End Date in SEVIS for a compelling academic or medical reason, for up to one additional year; (2) the student must separately file Form I-539 with USCIS to extend the actual admit-until date before it expires. A timely-filed I-539 buys up to 240 days of authorized stay while pending, but missing the filing deadline risks accruing unlawful presence. The I-539 EOS filing fee is reported at $420 if filed online / $470 by paper — a direct out-of-pocket cost that didn't exist under the old DSO-only SEVIS extension. See item 8 below: an incomplete I-539 package can now be denied outright rather than getting an RFE, so getting it right the first time matters more than before — and see item 4 below on USCIS's new authority to eventually mandate e-filing of this same form. USCIS will publish revised editions of Form I-539 and Form I-765 effective Sep 15, 2026, aligned to this new process. The current 08/28/24 edition of Form I-539 is accepted if postmarked or e-filed before Sep 15, 2026, but rejected on/after that date — only the new 09/15/26 edition is accepted from that point forward, so anyone filing right around the effective date should double-check which edition to use.

Practical risk: USCIS adjudication of Form I-539 is not instant, and the filing must be received before the current admission period expires. Combined with the deny-without-RFE policy (item 8), a late or incomplete filing is now higher-stakes than under the old D/S system.

New restrictions: F-1 graduate students are barred from changing programs, majors, or education levels once enrolled (narrow SEVP exceptions only); first-year undergraduates face a similar bar. DHS has discretion to postpone implementation of specifically these transfer/education-level provisions — not the core fixed-admission-period change — until as late as September 14, 2028, if it determines full implementation isn't feasible by the September 15, 2026 effective date.

Transition relief, with a hard outside limit: students in the U.S. under D/S before Sep 15, 2026 aren't immediately forced to file an EOS, and if they never leave and re-enter the U.S., they may remain until their I-20/OPT/STEM-OPT end date — but no later than Nov 14, 2030 (four years plus the 60-day grace period, measured from the Sep 15, 2026 effective date). If OPT/STEM OPT is part of the plan, Form I-765 (with I-539 bundled where applicable) must be filed by Mar 18, 2027 to use the simplified transition path. Leaving and re-entering the U.S. on or after Sep 15, 2026 ends the transition protections immediately — CBP will instead issue a date-specific Admit Until Date with only the new 30-day grace period.

Where things stand: the lawsuit filed Aug 18, 2026 by NAFSA and the Presidents' Alliance (see item 3 below) remains pending — case no. 1:26-cv-13799, now independently confirmed via an official court docket record — assigned to District Judge F. Dennis Saylor, IV, with an expedited schedule (DHS's response due Aug 31, 2026; motion hearing expected early September) but no ruling yet on the preliminary injunction motion that could pause the Sep 15, 2026 effective date if granted in time. As of this run (twelfth check), no Congressional Review Act disapproval resolution has been introduced despite the standard 60-day window that applies to "major rules." Treating the Sep 15, 2026 effective date as still on track pending any court action. SEVP has scheduled a stakeholder webinar for Aug 31, 2026 (2–3pm ET, no advance registration required — just log on, capped at 1,000 participants, recording and transcript posted afterward). USCIS also confirmed directly via its own newsroom alert that revised I-539/I-765 form editions publish Sep 15, 2026 with no grace period.

Virginia Tech specifically: Cranwell International Center (ISSS) has now published its first VT-specific implementation guidance — see the new item 1 above, including the Aug 31, 2026 deadline for graduate program/major changes and school transfers. Cranwell's dedicated Duration of Status Updates page and the general immigration_updates.html page continue to instruct students to keep submitting extension, CPT, OPT, STEM OPT, reduced-course-load, and degree-level-change requests through the IntlHokies Portal as normal for now — with D/S-specific questions directed to international@vt.edu. This is a federal regulation, so guidance from peer international offices (Yale, Cornell, Pitt, Colorado, UIC, Northeastern, WSU) applies identically. Consistent advice: talk to your DSO now if there's any chance of needing more time, so the SEVIS extension and USCIS filing aren't both rushed against a hard deadline.

Suggested next step: contact Cranwell International Center before Sep 15, 2026 to get the program end date extended in SEVIS while transition relief still applies, budget for the ~$420-470 I-539 fee, and make sure any I-539/I-765 package is complete on first submission given the deny-without-RFE policy below.

3. Coalition sues DHS, seeks preliminary injunction to block the D/S rule — now confirmed via official court docket, still no ruling

Confirmed (official) · first tracked 2026-08-19 · filed 2026-08-18 · upgraded to confirmed 2026-08-25 · re-checked 2026-08-25

On August 18, 2026, a coalition — NAFSA: Association of International Educators, the Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration, the Association of Independent Colleges and Universities in Massachusetts (AICUM), the American Federation of Teachers (AFT), the Graduate Labor Organization (AFT Local 6516), the UAW, UAW Local 2322, and The NewsGuild-CWA — filed a complaint and motion for preliminary injunction in the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts. The suit challenges DHS's July 17, 2026 final rule ending Duration of Status (item 2 above) under the Administrative Procedure Act, arguing DHS failed to adequately assess the rule's costs and benefits, meaningfully respond to public comments, consider less burdensome alternatives, or justify the rule based on its stated objectives, and that DHS also provided an inadequate public comment period and exceeded its statutory authority. Plaintiffs, represented by law firm McDermott Will & Schulte, ask the court to stay and vacate the rule outright, or at minimum to enjoin its enforcement against plaintiffs and their members, before the rule's Sep 15, 2026 effective date. The case is assigned to District Judge F. Dennis Saylor, IV.

Mass.), reported directly by Bloomberg Law and American Bazaar and matching the caption on the as-filed Complaint and preliminary-injunction motion PDFs, which the plaintiff coalition itself hosts (Case 1:26-cv-13799, Document 1, filed 08/18/26).

4. USCIS interim final rule enables mandatory electronic filing — I-539/I-765 reportedly in scope

Confirmed (official) · first tracked 2026-08-18 · effective 2026-08-11 · re-checked 2026-08-25

USCIS published an interim final rule (91 FR 51924, Federal Register doc. 2026-16313), effective August 11, 2026, that gives the agency legal authority to require mandatory electronic filing ("e-filing") of certain benefit requests. The rule itself doesn't immediately force any specific form online: USCIS can only mandate e-filing for a form once it has been available for e-filing for at least 180 days, and must give the public at least 60 days' advance notice before a mandate for a specific form or filing circumstance actually takes effect. Pending petitions are unaffected. Secondary legal-industry coverage lists an initial set of roughly 22 forms USCIS is expected to phase toward mandatory e-filing — reportedly including Form I-129, I-130, N-400, I-90, I-131, and, notably for F-1 students, Form I-539 (Extension/Change of Nonimmigrant Status) and Form I-765 (Employment Authorization).

Why it matters here: Form I-539 is exactly the form F-1 students will need to file for an Extension of Stay (EOS) once the Sep 15, 2026 fixed-admission-period rule (item 2 above) takes effect, and Form I-765 is the OPT/STEM OPT work-authorization form. If and when USCIS issues the required 60-day notice mandating e-filing for these two forms specifically, paper filing could stop being accepted for them — worth watching alongside the deny-without-RFE policy (item 8 below), since an incomplete e-filed package would carry the same denial risk as an incomplete paper one.

Confirmation status: confirmed directly via the Federal Register document itself and a matching official USCIS newsroom alert, "USCIS To Require Electronic Filing of Forms: Strengthening National Security." No form-specific 60-day mandate notice (for I-539, I-765, or any other form) has been published yet — only the enabling framework and the interim final rule itself exist so far, unchanged as of today's re-check (eighth check). Public comments on the rule are open through Oct 13, 2026.

6. ICE/HSI nationwide OPT fraud crackdown — 10,000+ students flagged, now confirmed via Federal Register

Confirmed (official) · first tracked 2026-08-16 · press conference 2026-05-12 · upgraded to confirmed 2026-08-25 · re-checked 2026-08-25

At a May 12, 2026 press conference, Acting ICE Director Todd Lyons and Acting HSI Associate Director John Condon announced that federal investigators had identified more than 10,000 F-1/STEM OPT students linked to "highly suspect employers," drawn from a review of the top 25 OPT-employing companies nationwide. HSI site visits across eight states (Texas, Virginia, Georgia, Illinois, New York, New Jersey, North Carolina, Florida) reportedly found empty buildings and locked doors, residential addresses listed as worksites, offshore payroll and HR arrangements, and shell-company structures. In one Northern Texas case, an employer's paperwork claimed 500 OPT students but only 3 actual employees were found on-site. Lyons called the findings "only the tip of the iceberg."

Upgraded to Confirmed (official), 2026-08-25: the DHS final rule's own preamble (Federal Register doc. 2026-14439, published Jul 17, 2026) directly cites Lyons' May 12, 2026 statement — "identified more than 10,000 cases of potential fraud associated with the OPT program" — as part of the rule's official justification for ending Duration of Status. Because this figure is now sourced to an official Federal Register document rather than only press accounts of the press conference, this item moves from "Reported, unconfirmed" to Confirmed (official).

Why it matters here: a student weighing an extra semester is very often also counting on post-completion OPT or STEM OPT to work afterward. This crackdown doesn't change the OPT regulation itself, but it signals a real enforcement risk: a student whose employer gets flagged as "suspect" — even through no fault of their own — could face SEVIS or work-authorization complications. Worth watching for follow-on actions like SEVIS terminations or EAD revocations tied to this specific investigation.
