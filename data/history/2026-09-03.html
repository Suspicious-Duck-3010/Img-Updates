3. DHS Final Rule ending Duration of Status

Confirmed (official) · first tracked 2026-08-10 · last updated 2026-09-02 · re-checked 2026-09-03

For decades, F-1 students were admitted for "Duration of Status" (D/S) — their legal stay tracked their I-20 program dates, and a Designated School Official (DSO) could extend that end date directly in SEVIS for a compelling academic or medical reason, with no separate federal filing required. The new rule ends that. Beginning September 15, 2026, F-1 students receive a fixed "Admit Until Date" on their I-94, set to the length of their program as listed on the I-20 — capped at four years for undergraduate and doctoral programs (24 months for language-training programs) — plus a 30-day grace period to arrive and another 30 days after program completion.

Why this matters for someone who might need an extra semester: there are now two separate actions — (1) the DSO can still extend the Program End Date in SEVIS for a compelling academic or medical reason, for up to one additional year; (2) the student must separately file Form I-539 with USCIS to extend the actual admit-until date before it expires. A timely-filed I-539 buys up to 240 days of authorized stay while pending, but missing the filing deadline risks accruing unlawful presence. The I-539 EOS filing fee is reported at $420 if filed online / $470 by paper — a direct out-of-pocket cost that didn't exist under the old DSO-only SEVIS extension. See item 9 below: an incomplete I-539 package can now be denied outright rather than getting an RFE, so getting it right the first time matters more than before — and see item 5 below on USCIS's new authority to eventually mandate e-filing of this same form. USCIS will publish revised editions of Form I-539 and Form I-765 effective Sep 15, 2026, aligned to this new process. The current 08/28/24 edition of Form I-539 is accepted if postmarked or e-filed before Sep 15, 2026, but rejected on/after that date — only the new 09/15/26 edition is accepted from that point forward, so anyone filing right around the effective date should double-check which edition to use.

Practical risk: USCIS adjudication of Form I-539 is not instant, and the filing must be received before the current admission period expires. Combined with the deny-without-RFE policy (item 9), a late or incomplete filing is now higher-stakes than under the old D/S system.

New restrictions: F-1 graduate students are barred from changing programs, majors, or education levels once enrolled (narrow SEVP exceptions only); first-year undergraduates face a similar bar. DHS has discretion to postpone implementation of specifically these transfer/education-level provisions — not the core fixed-admission-period change — until as late as September 14, 2028, if it determines full implementation isn't feasible by the September 15, 2026 effective date.

Transition relief, with a hard outside limit: students in the U.S. under D/S before Sep 15, 2026 aren't immediately forced to file an EOS, and if they never leave and re-enter the U.S., they may remain until their I-20/OPT/STEM-OPT end date — but no later than Nov 14, 2030 (four years plus the 60-day grace period, measured from the Sep 15, 2026 effective date). If OPT/STEM OPT is part of the plan, Form I-765 (with I-539 bundled where applicable) must be filed by Mar 18, 2027 to use the simplified transition path. Leaving and re-entering the U.S. on or after Sep 15, 2026 ends the transition protections immediately — CBP will instead issue a date-specific Admit Until Date with only the new 30-day grace period.

Where things stand: the lawsuit filed Aug 18, 2026 by NAFSA and the Presidents' Alliance (see item 4 below) remains pending — case no. 1:26-cv-13799, confirmed via an official court docket record — assigned to District Judge F. Dennis Saylor, IV, with an expedited schedule (DHS's response was due Aug 31, 2026 — no report found yet confirming whether DHS actually filed) but no ruling yet on the preliminary injunction motion that could pause the Sep 15, 2026 effective date if granted in time. Update today: yesterday's motion-hearing-date discrepancy (Sep 3 vs. Sep 9) is now resolved in favor of Sep 3, 2026, 11:00 AM ET — today — via a second independent, non-party docket source; see item 4 below. No Congressional Review Act disapproval resolution has been found as of this run. Treating the Sep 15, 2026 effective date as still on track pending any court action. SEVP will deploy SEVIS Release 6.89.1 the night before the rule takes effect — Sep 14, 2026, 8:00 PM ET through Sep 15, 2026, 12:00 AM ET. Inside Higher Ed's Aug 24, 2026 report that 15 colleges/university systems have submitted court declarations documenting real disenrollments and application drops already tied to this rule remains the most notable recent litigation-adjacent development — see item 13 below (still unconfirmed against the primary court record). USCIS also confirmed directly via its own newsroom alert that revised I-539/I-765 form editions publish Sep 15, 2026 with no grace period.

Virginia Tech specifically: Cranwell International Center (ISSS) has now published its first VT-specific implementation guidance — see the new item 2 above, including the Aug 31, 2026 deadline for graduate program/major changes and school transfers. Cranwell's dedicated Duration of Status Updates page and the general immigration_updates.html page continue to instruct students to keep submitting extension, CPT, OPT, STEM OPT, reduced-course-load, and degree-level-change requests through the IntlHokies Portal as normal for now — with D/S-specific questions directed to international@vt.edu. 4. Coalition sues DHS, seeks preliminary injunction to block the D/S rule — hearing is TODAY (Sep 3, discrepancy resolved), DHS response deadline has passed

Confirmed (official) · first tracked 2026-08-19 · filed 2026-08-18 · upgraded to confirmed 2026-08-25 · re-checked 2026-09-03

On August 18, 2026, a coalition — NAFSA: Association of International Educators, the Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration, the Association of Independent Colleges and Universities in Massachusetts (AICUM), the American Federation of Teachers (AFT), the Graduate Labor Organization (AFT Local 6516), the UAW, UAW Local 2322, and The NewsGuild-CWA — filed a complaint and motion for preliminary injunction in the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts. The suit challenges DHS's July 17, 2026 final rule ending Duration of Status (item 3 above) under the Administrative Procedure Act, arguing DHS failed to adequately assess the rule's costs and benefits, meaningfully respond to public comments, consider less burdensome alternatives, or justify the rule based on its stated objectives, and that DHS also provided an inadequate public comment period and exceeded its statutory authority. Plaintiffs, represented by law firm McDermott Will & Schulte, ask the court to stay and vacate the rule outright, or at minimum to enjoin its enforcement against plaintiffs and their members, before the rule's Sep 15, 2026 effective date. The case is assigned to District Judge F. Dennis Saylor, IV.
