1. UPDATED: Government's First Circuit appeal now docketed as No. 26-2112 -- Oct 9 deadline for both sides' "next steps" positions still pending; injunction remains in force

UpdatedMajorConfirmed · first tracked 2026-08-19 · last updated 2026-10-06 · re-checked 2026-10-06

On Sept 14, 2026 -- one day before the D/S-ending final rule's Sept 15 effective date -- U.S. District Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV (D. Mass.) granted nationwide preliminary relief in Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration et al. v. DHS, 1:26-cv-13799 (D. Mass.), the coalition suit filed Aug 18, 2026 by NAFSA, the Presidents' Alliance, AICUM, AFT, GLO, UAW/UAW Local 2322 and The NewsGuild-CWA. The order blocks DHS and ICE from implementing the rule nationwide (not just for plaintiffs' own members) while the case proceeds, and the court reportedly wrote that 'the promulgation of the rule clearly failed to comply with the [Administrative Procedure Act],' calling the administration's stated national-security rationale weak and questioning whether the rule's real purpose was something else. As a result, F, J, and I nonimmigrants -- including F-1 students -- continue to be admitted for duration of status (D/S) for now, and the planned shift to a fixed admission period (capped at 4 years, tied to the I-20/DS-2019 program end date, requiring a separate Form I-539 extension-of-stay adjudication to go beyond it) has NOT taken effect. This is directly the scenario this tracker exists to watch: the enjoined rule is exactly the mechanism (a hard, USCIS-adjudicated admission-period cap) that would have squeezed a VT student needing an extra semester or two to finish. For now, the older, more forgiving D/S framework (extend by simply getting a new Program End Date on the I-20 from the DSO, no separate USCIS filing or adjudication needed just to keep pursuing 'normal progress') remains in place. IMPORTANT CAVEATS: (1) this is a PRELIMINARY injunction, not a final ruling -- the underlying legality of the rule has not been decided; (2) the government is widely expected to seek an emergency stay of the injunction and/or appeal to the First Circuit, and immigration counsel are advising students and employers to be prepared for the rule to be reinstated 'with little or no notice' if a higher court disagrees; (3) separately, and confusingly, USCIS and SEVP proceeded on Sept 14-15 with technical rollout tied to the now-enjoined rule anyway -- see new item 'ds-rule-operational-rollout-2026-09' -- so DSOs/students should watch for conflicting signals about which forms/processes currently apply. 4. UPDATED: SEVP CPT crackdown now spans 15+ named institutions -- MIT and Harvard confirmed added alongside Virginia Tech, UC Berkeley, UCLA, Penn, Rochester and others

UpdatedConfirmed · first tracked 2026-08-15 · last updated 2026-10-04 · re-checked 2026-10-06

On Aug 12, 2026, SEVP issued Broadcast Message 2608-01, 'Reminder of Liability for DSOs Regarding CPT Authorization,' to every PDSO/DSO at SEVP-certified schools. SEVP says it has observed a rise in Curricular Practical Training (CPT) authorizations that do not meet regulatory requirements -- specifically CPT not constituting an integral part of an established curriculum -- and is increasing scrutiny of CPT approvals; the message states DSOs should only authorize CPT where its absence 'would make the attainment of a degree an impossibility.' The message does not change the CPT regulation itself and DHS's July D/S final rule separately stated it 'does not prohibit or eliminate Day-1 CPT' and 'does not make substantive changes to CPT.' But it signals stepped-up enforcement: schools found authorizing CPT improperly risk investigation and loss of SEVP certification. Washington State University's Aug 7, 2026 international-office advisory announced ISSS there will stop approving full-time CPT during the academic year starting Spring 2027, except for programs with an explicit mandatory-internship curriculum requirement -- an early example of a university preemptively tightening its own CPT approval practices. No indication yet that Virginia Tech's Cranwell International Center has announced a similar CPT-specific change; VT's CPT page still describes standard part-time/full-time CPT eligibility rules with no dated policy update, though VT's new provost/CIC guidance (see item 'vt-provost-cic-guidance-2026-08') does not mention CPT specifically either. NEW (2026-09-07) -- VIRGINIA TECH HAS NOW RESPONDED: Cranwell's own CPT page (international.vt.edu/employment/cpt.html) now states that Cranwell has paused new CPT authorizations while it evaluates SEVP's Aug 2026 broadcast messages (BCM 2608-01 and 2608-02), with an exception for programs where practical training is a required, non-elective part of the established curriculum -- meaning it appears on the student's official graduation checklist for their major and is required of every student in that program regardless of F-1 status, per the university catalog. Students who already hold CPT authorization for the current semester are not affected by the pause. Cranwell is compiling a comprehensive list of programs/courses that meet this threshold and has asked students not to submit new CPT requests until that list is published; students with currently pending CPT requests in the IntlHokies Portal will receive individual communication about their specific case. This is the first Virginia-Tech-specific CPT policy response this tracker has found since BCM 2608-01 was issued on Aug 12, 2026.
