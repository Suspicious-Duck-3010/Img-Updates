Detailed writeup

1. DHS Final Rule ending Duration of Status

Confirmed (official) · first tracked 2026-08-10 · last updated 2026-08-10

For decades, F-1 students were admitted for "Duration of Status" (D/S) — their legal stay tracked their I-20 program dates, and a Designated School Official (DSO) could extend that end date directly in SEVIS for a compelling academic or medical reason, with no separate federal filing required. The new rule ends that. Beginning September 15, 2026, F-1 students receive a fixed "Admit Until Date" on their I-94, set to the length of their program as listed on the I-20 — capped at four years for undergraduate and doctoral programs (24 months for language-training programs) — plus a 30-day grace period to arrive and another 30 days after program completion.

Why this matters for someone who might need an extra semester: there are now two separate actions — (1) the DSO can still extend the Program End Date in SEVIS for a compelling academic or medical reason, for up to one additional year; (2) the student must separately file Form I-539 with USCIS to extend the actual admit-until date before it expires. A timely-filed I-539 buys up to 240 days of authorized stay while pending, but missing the filing deadline risks accruing unlawful presence.

Practical risk: USCIS adjudication of Form I-539 is not instant, and the filing must be received before the current admission period expires. Combined with the new RFE/NOID policy below, a late or incomplete filing is now higher-stakes than under the old D/S system.

New restrictions: F-1 graduate students are barred from changing programs, majors, or education levels once enrolled (narrow SEVP exceptions only); first-year undergraduates face a similar bar. Relevant if an extra semester is being used to also switch a concentration or degree level.

Transition relief: students in the U.S. under D/S before Sep 15, 2026 aren't immediately forced to file an EOS, but if OPT/STEM OPT is part of the plan, Form I-765 (with I-539 bundled where applicable) must be filed by Mar 18, 2027 to use the simplified transition path.

Where things stand: NAFSA and higher-ed associations have publicly opposed the rule and NAFSA's CEO said the association will "explore every available avenue" to challenge it, but as of this run no lawsuit has been filed and no CRA disapproval resolution has passed. SEVP has scheduled a stakeholder webinar for Aug 31, 2026 (2–3pm ET) to walk through the rule — reported via a secondary source since studyinthestates.dhs.gov could not be reached directly this run.

This is a federal regulation, so guidance from peer international offices (Yale, Cornell, Pitt, Colorado) applies identically. 2. NEW — USCIS: deny-without-RFE/NOID policy (PA-2026-05)

Reported, unconfirmed · first tracked 2026-08-10 · effective (reported) 2026-08-05

Multiple immigration-law sources report that USCIS updated Policy Manual guidance, effective August 5, 2026, giving adjudicators discretion to deny a benefit request that lacks required initial evidence or otherwise fails to establish eligibility — without first issuing a Request for Evidence (RFE) or Notice of Intent to Deny (NOID). Officers may also set RFE response deadlines shorter than the prior 12-week maximum. USCIS reportedly frames this as a response to incomplete or placeholder filings driving adjudication delays.

Why it matters here: under the new fixed-admission-period regime, F-1 students needing an extra semester must file Form I-539 (Extension of Stay) — and OPT/STEM OPT transition filers use Form I-765. A materially incomplete package can now be denied outright rather than getting a chance to supplement the record, raising the cost of filing errors right when more students will be filing EOS applications for the first time.

3. NEW — DHS regulatory review of OPT/STEM OPT (RIN 1653-AA97)

Reported, unconfirmed · first tracked 2026-08-10

DHS confirmed in a January 9, 2026 letter that it is re-evaluating the scope and duration of Optional Practical Training (OPT), including STEM OPT, citing fraud, national-security, and worker-displacement concerns. A rulemaking entry (RIN 1653-AA97) appears in the Spring 2026 Unified Regulatory Agenda. As of today, no Notice of Proposed Rulemaking has been published in the Federal Register; secondary reporting suggests any actual proposal is unlikely before early 2027.

Why it matters here: a VT student weighing an extra semester is often also counting on post-completion OPT or STEM OPT timing to work or gain experience afterward. No concrete rule exists yet, but this is worth watching alongside the D/S transition deadline (Mar 18, 2027 above), since both could interact for students finishing later than originally planned.
