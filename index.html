1. Federal court grants nationwide preliminary injunction blocking the D/S-ending final rule, one business day before its Sept 15 effective date -- DHS/ICE barred from implementing the rule while litigation continues; government expected to appeal

Confirmed · first tracked 2026-08-19 · last updated 2026-10-02 · re-checked 2026-10-02

On Sept 14, 2026 -- one day before the D/S-ending final rule's Sept 15 effective date -- U.S. District Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV (D. Mass.) granted nationwide preliminary relief in Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration et al. v. DHS, 1:26-cv-13799 (D. Mass.), the coalition suit filed Aug 18, 2026 by NAFSA, the Presidents' Alliance, AICUM, AFT, GLO, UAW/UAW Local 2322 and The NewsGuild-CWA. The order blocks DHS and ICE from implementing the rule nationwide (not just for plaintiffs' own members) while the case proceeds, and the court reportedly wrote that 'the promulgation of the rule clearly failed to comply with the [Administrative Procedure Act],' calling the administration's stated national-security rationale weak and questioning whether the rule's real purpose was something else. As a result, F, J, and I nonimmigrants -- including F-1 students -- continue to be admitted for duration of status (D/S) for now, and the planned shift to a fixed admission period (capped at 4 years, tied to the I-20/DS-2019 program end date, requiring a separate Form I-539 extension-of-stay adjudication to go beyond it) has NOT taken effect. This is directly the scenario this tracker exists to watch: the enjoined rule is exactly the mechanism (a hard, USCIS-adjudicated admission-period cap) that would have squeezed a VT student needing an extra semester or two to finish. For now, the older, more forgiving D/S framework (extend by simply getting a new Program End Date on the I-20 from the DSO, no separate USCIS filing or adjudication needed just to keep pursuing 'normal progress') remains in place. IMPORTANT CAVEATS: (1) this is a PRELIMINARY injunction, not a final ruling -- the underlying legality of the rule has not been decided; (2) the government is widely expected to seek an emergency stay of the injunction and/or appeal to the First Circuit, and immigration counsel are advising students and employers to be prepared for the rule to be reinstated 'with little or no notice' if a higher court disagrees; (3) separately, and confusingly, USCIS and SEVP proceeded on Sept 14-15 with technical rollout tied to the now-enjoined rule anyway -- see new item 'ds-rule-operational-rollout-2026-09' -- so DSOs/students should watch for conflicting signals about which forms/processes currently apply. This tracker will keep monitoring for (a) any DHS emergency-stay motion or appeal, (b) any Federal Register notice postponing/updating the rule's status, and (c) Cranwell/VT guidance responding specifically to the injunction.

2. NEW: Virginia Tech Provost/Cranwell issue formal guidance on the D/S rule -- Aug 31, 2026 deadline for grad program/major changes and school transfers

Confirmed · first tracked 2026-08-22 · last updated 2026-10-02 · re-checked 2026-10-02

Virginia Tech's Office of the Provost and the Cranwell International Center (CIC) published formal guidance for VT faculty and staff on anticipated campus impacts of the Sep 15, 2026 DHS Duration-of-Status final rule (see item 'd-s-final-rule'). Key points directly relevant to a student who may need an extra semester: (1) all F-1/J-1 students will face increased external (USCIS-level) review of timely progress toward a degree, with any program extension now going through what VT describes as a more heavily monitored extension process; (2) students who complete one program after Sep 15, 2026 are barred from starting another program at the same or a lower level (e.g., master's-to-master's is out; master's-to-PhD remains allowed), matching the federal rule; (3) graduate students will be unable to transfer institutions or change programs until their current degree is completed; (4) undergraduates will be unable to transfer schools or change their declared major during their first year of study. Most actionable for students right now: Cranwell has set a hard internal deadline of Monday, Aug 31, 2026 for all paperwork requesting a program/major change, a program-level change (e.g., PhD to master's), or a school transfer -- submissions, including advisor comments, must reach CIC by that date to be processed under the current (pre-Sep-15) rules; this can run concurrently with the Graduate School's own approval process. This is the first Virginia-Tech-specific implementation guidance found since the D/S final rule was published on Jul 17, 2026; Cranwell's general 'Duration of Status Updates' page had previously said only that the university was still reviewing impacts with no VT-specific procedural guidance. New detail found today (2026-08-23), from Cranwell's own Duration of Status Updates page: extension requests are not reviewed until after the first day of classes in a student's final term, regardless of the reason for the request, and Cranwell states it will only be reviewing extension requests for students with a Fall 2026 program end date (Fall 2026 classes end Wed, Dec 9, 2026; degree conferral Wed, Dec 16, 2026) -- a student whose program end date falls later (e.g. Spring 2027 or beyond) is not yet described as eligible to have an extension request reviewed under this guidance. New today (2026-08-25): Cranwell is holding mandatory 'D/S Basics' webinars for all F-1/J-1 advisees on Tue, Aug 25, 2026, 7:00 PM ET (today) and Fri, Aug 28, 2026, 9:00 AM ET, ahead of the Aug 31, 2026 paperwork deadline and the Sep 15, 2026 rule effective date.

NEW (2026-10-02): Cranwell's Duration of Status Updates page now states that any F-1/J-1 student who missed the mandatory 'D/S Basics' sessions held in late August is required to complete a self-paced 'D/S Basics Course' instead, and that optional D/S drop-in sessions are replacing Cranwell's regular Tuesday walk-in advising through early October 2026. Cranwell also reiterates that SEVIS itself is still undergoing system maintenance tied to the (currently enjoined) elimination of Duration of Status, which continues to cause delays on eForm requests outside Cranwell's control. No new deadline beyond the previously reported Aug 31, 2026 paperwork cutoff was found; this is an operational/advising-capacity update, not a change to the underlying guidance.

4. UPDATED: SEVP CPT crackdown keeps spreading -- USF, Cornell, and Princeton now tightening CPT alongside Virginia Tech, WSU, Boston University, USC, and U. Washington

Confirmed · first tracked 2026-08-15 · last updated 2026-10-02 · re-checked 2026-10-02

On Aug 12, 2026, SEVP issued Broadcast Message 2608-01, 'Reminder of Liability for DSOs Regarding CPT Authorization,' to every PDSO/DSO at SEVP-certified schools. SEVP says it has observed a rise in Curricular Practical Training (CPT) authorizations that do not meet regulatory requirements -- specifically CPT not constituting an integral part of an established curriculum -- and is increasing scrutiny of CPT approvals; the message states DSOs should only authorize CPT where its absence 'would make the attainment of a degree an impossibility.' The message does not change the CPT regulation itself and DHS's July D/S final rule separately stated it 'does not prohibit or eliminate Day-1 CPT' and 'does not make substantive changes to CPT.' But it signals stepped-up enforcement: schools found authorizing CPT improperly risk investigation and loss of SEVP certification. Washington State University's Aug 7, 2026 international-office advisory announced ISSS there will stop approving full-time CPT during the academic year starting Spring 2027, except for programs with an explicit mandatory-internship curriculum requirement -- an early example of a university preemptively tightening its own CPT approval practices. No indication yet that Virginia Tech's Cranwell International Center has announced a similar CPT-specific change; VT's CPT page still describes standard part-time/full-time CPT eligibility rules with no dated policy update, though VT's new provost/CIC guidance (see item 'vt-provost-cic-guidance-2026-08') does not mention CPT specifically either. NEW (2026-09-07) -- VIRGINIA TECH HAS NOW RESPONDED: Cranwell's own CPT page (international.vt.edu/employment/cpt.html) now states that Cranwell has paused new CPT authorizations while it evaluates SEVP's Aug 2026 broadcast messages (BCM 2608-01 and 2608-02), with an exception for programs where practical training is a required, non-elective part of the established curriculum -- meaning it appears on the student's official graduation checklist for their major and is required of every student in that program regardless of F-1 status, per the university catalog. Students who already hold CPT authorization for the current semester are not affected by the pause. Cranwell is compiling a comprehensive list of programs/courses that meet this threshold and has asked students not to submit new CPT requests until that list is published; students with currently pending CPT requests in the IntlHokies Portal will receive individual communication about their specific case. This is the first Virginia-Tech-specific CPT policy response this tracker has found since BCM 2608-01 was issued on Aug 12, 2026. NEW (2026-08-26): SEVP issued a follow-up Broadcast Message, BCM 2608-02, on Aug 24, 2026, titled 'Guidance for Designated School Officials Regarding [CPT authorization]' -- a direct follow-up to BCM 2608-01. It sharpens the compliance test schools must apply: CPT is only permitted where the training is genuinely an integral part of an established curriculum, and SEVP flags specific patterns it has observed as noncompliant, including schools describing CPT as optional rather than integral, and schools not requiring all students in the same established curriculum to complete the same practical-training component despite the regulation requiring that consistency. The message reiterates that failure to comply can put a school's SEVP certification at risk. This does not change the underlying CPT regulation (Day-1 CPT itself remains permitted per DHS's July D/S rule), but it is a second, more detailed enforcement signal within two weeks -- underscoring that DSOs nationwide, including at Virginia Tech, are under growing federal pressure to tighten CPT authorization practices. NEW (2026-09-25): the CPT pause triggered by SEVP's Aug 12/24, 2026 broadcast messages has now spread well beyond Virginia Tech and Washington State -- Boston University, USC, and the University of Washington have also paused or narrowed new CPT authorizations for programs where practical training isn't a required, non-elective curriculum component, while continuing to process CPT that is required for graduation. Re-checked 2026-10-01 (thirty-sixth-ish check): three more universities confirmed tightening CPT in direct response to SEVP's Aug 12/24, 2026 broadcast messages -- the University of South Florida (per The Oracle, Sep 29, 2026) reports USF's International Student & Scholar Services office adopted 'a very strict process' with added requirements for academic departments to verify CPT coursework counts toward graduation; Cornell (per the Cornell Daily Sun) announced tighter temporary-work-eligibility criteria for some international students following the ICE messages; and Princeton (per the Daily Princetonian) restricted internship/CPT approvals following the same federal guidance. This brings the number of named institutions responding to BCM 2608-01/2608-02 to at least eight (Virginia Tech, Washington State, Boston University, USC, University of Washington, USF, Cornell, Princeton), reinforcing that this is now a broad, national pattern of schools narrowing CPT to only curriculum-required, non-elective training. A targeted site:ice.gov search again found no CPT broadcast message newer than BCM 2608-02 (Aug 24, 2026). No new Virginia-Tech-specific update found today; Cranwell's pause and qualifying-programs-list process remain as previously described.

NEW (2026-10-02): Carleton College is newly identified (via The Carletonian student paper) as having paused Curricular Practical Training (CPT) authorizations for international students following SEVP's Aug 12/24, 2026 broadcast messages (BCM 2608-01/2608-02), joining the growing list of institutions tightening CPT in direct response to the federal guidance. This brings the number of named institutions responding to at least nine: Virginia Tech, Washington State, Boston University, USC, University of Washington, USF, Cornell, Princeton, and now Carleton College. A targeted site:ice.gov search again found no SEVP broadcast message newer than BCM 2608-02 (Aug 24, 2026); no new Virginia-Tech-specific CPT update found today beyond the existing pause and qualifying-programs-list process.

5. USCIS Interim Final Rule enabling mandatory electronic filing (e-Filing) -- covers I-539/I-765 among forms in scope

Confirmed · first tracked 2026-08-18 · last updated 2026-10-02 · re-checked 2026-10-02

USCIS published an interim final rule (91 FR 51924, doc. 2026-16313), effective Aug 11, 2026, giving USCIS authority to require mandatory electronic filing (e-filing) of certain benefit requests. The rule itself does not immediately force any specific form online -- USCIS may only mandate e-filing for a form once that form has been available for e-filing for at least 180 days, and must give at least 60 days' advance public notice before a mandate for a specific form/filing circumstance takes effect. It does not affect petitions already pending. Secondary coverage lists an initial set of roughly 22 forms USCIS is expected to phase toward mandatory e-filing, reportedly including I-129, I-130, N-400, I-90, I-131, and -- notably -- I-539 (Extension/Change of Nonimmigrant Status) and I-765 (Employment Authorization). This directly matters for F-1 students who will need to file Form I-539 for an Extension of Stay (EOS) once the Sep 15, 2026 fixed-admission-period rule takes effect, and for I-765 OPT/STEM OPT applicants: if/when USCIS issues the required 60-day notice mandating e-filing for these forms, paper filing could stop being accepted for them. Public comments on the interim final rule are open through Oct 13, 2026.

UPDATE (2026-10-02): press coverage clarifies that DHS issued this interim final rule without advance public comment but is accepting comments on it through Oct 13, 2026, and that USCIS must post a notice at least 60 days before making e-filing mandatory for any specific form (after that form has been available for e-filing at least 180 days) -- meaning the earliest any form, including I-539 or I-765, could actually become e-filing-only is around October 2026 at the very earliest, and only if USCIS had already posted the required notice, which has not been reported. As of this run, no form has been confirmed mandatory e-filing-only yet.

Latest check (2026-10-02): Re-checked 2026-10-02: minor update -- comment period on the interim final rule runs through Oct 13, 2026; no form has yet been confirmed as mandatory e-filing-only.

6. UPGRADED TO CONFIRMED (procedural): reginfo.gov officially confirms OIRA concluded review of DHS's OPT-fee rule (RIN 1653-AB01) on Sept 11, 2026 -- dollar amount still unofficial

Confirmed · first tracked 2026-08-12 · last updated 2026-10-01 · re-checked 2026-10-02

Multiple outlets report the administration is weighing a $100,000 fee applicable to OPT employment authorization (and by extension STEM OPT), modeled on the now-struck-down $100,000 H-1B fee proclamation. As of this run, no signed proclamation or DHS published rule/fee schedule has been reported for an OPT fee -- unlike the H-1B fee, which was signed via proclamation on Sep 19, 2025 and then struck down by Judge Sorokin on Jun 8, 2026. Note: a SEPARATE H-1B petition-fee rule (RIN 1615-AD20) cleared White House/OIRA review on Aug 19, 2026 -- this is a distinct H-1B-specific rulemaking, not an OPT fee, and should not be conflated with the reported $100K OPT fee tracked in this item. Immigration attorneys quoted say an OPT fee is unlikely to survive legal challenge absent Congressional authorization, citing that H-1B precedent. Who would pay (student/university/employer) remains unresolved; USCIS has not published regulatory language explaining the proposal, coverage, exemptions, or administration.
